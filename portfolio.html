01Indie Games · Eldritch House · Nov 2025 – Present

Shadow Sacrament: The Roots of Evil — Early-Stage Indie Game Research

Role
Embedded UX Researcher — moderated playtests, localization audit, and cross-session synthesis
Context
Embedded research partner to a Brazil-based indie studio (published by DANGEN Entertainment)

Ongoing embedded research on Shadow Sacrament, a survival horror indie game. Work spans moderated playtests routed by a performance-based participant framework, a frame-by-frame PT-BR localization audit, and same-day escalation of critical bugs to the dev team — including a keybind remapping issue that decoupled animation from function and PT-BR text bleeding into the English locale.

Moderated PlaytestingLocalization AuditParticipant Routing FrameworksSession ReportingBug EscalationIndie Games
What problem were you solving?
Shadow Sacrament needed structured playtest data early enough to inform design decisions rather than validate them after the fact — and needed it delivered with enough care that a small, resource-constrained team could act on it without feeling attacked.
What methods did you use and why?
A performance-based participant routing framework used non-boss death count to route players into the right test track. A five-version testing framework, moderated session scripts, and continuation session scripts kept sessions consistent across a growing participant pool spanning multiple countries and input methods.
What did you find?
The localization audit surfaced 7 findings including untranslated map room labels, inventory tab labels, and a completion screen. A pause menu cursor finding and two technical bugs were escalated directly to developers — one reviewed and confirmed the same day it was delivered.
What would you do differently?
I'd formalize the recurring-findings rule earlier — flagging an issue once until the team responds, then moving it to an appendix rather than repeating it prominently in every report, so priority signal doesn't get diluted as the study continues across Acts.
Reflection

Working with a small studio taught me that the delivery of a finding matters as much as the finding itself. Framing research as serving the game — before testing even begins — and acknowledging design intent before describing where it broke down for players kept the relationship collaborative rather than adversarial, even when the news wasn't good.

02Independent Study · Disney Speedstorm · May 2024

Disney Speedstorm — Independent UX Research Study

Role
Independent researcher — study design, participant recruitment, facilitation, and synthesis
Context
Independent observational study conducted via PlaytestCloud credits received upon completing Steve Bromley's Games User Research Fundamentals course

An independent observational study on Disney Speedstorm, a live-service mobile racing game by Gameloft, conducted as a practical application of the Games User Research Fundamentals course. Five moderated think-aloud sessions across 4 countries, focused on new player onboarding, progression comprehension, and early-game friction.

Think-Aloud PlaytestingObservational ResearchCross-Session SynthesisBehavioral AnnotationMental Model AnalysisLive-Service GamesNew Player Onboarding
What problem were you solving?
As a practical exercise from the Games User Research Fundamentals course, I wanted to apply end-to-end research methodology to a real live-service game. Disney Speedstorm offered a rich onboarding context — a game with strong brand appeal but a complex progression economy that new players would need to navigate from session one.
What methods did you use and why?
Moderated think-aloud sessions via PlaytestCloud let me observe real new-player behavior without studio access. Five sessions across a diverse participant pool (ages 17-36, 4 countries) gave enough signal to identify cross-participant patterns. I developed a behavioral annotation system using tags to structure cross-session synthesis.
What did you find?
The most consistent cross-participant finding was confusion around in-game currency and reward systems — flagged independently by 4 of 5 participants regardless of age or gaming experience. Onboarding clarity split clearly by player archetype: experienced kart-racing players found it sufficient; genre-unfamiliar players did not.
What would you do differently?
With more time I would have recruited specifically for Disney-primary players to better understand the experience gap for that audience, and designed a second evaluative round to test whether specific changes addressed the currency confusion finding.
Reflection

Running an independent study on a live production game without studio access sharpened my ability to extract signal from purely observational methods. The most valuable outcome wasn't any single finding but the realization that cross-participant pattern recognition is where the real research judgment lives: one player struggling with currency is noise; four players independently flagging the same system is a finding worth acting on.

03Web3 / Developer UX · Kadena · Oct 2022 – May 2024

Kadena (K-Block) — Blockchain Developer Onboarding

Role
Volunteer UX research contributor (Associate, then Lead) — survey research, workflow mapping, and synthesis workshops
Team Context
Collaborative volunteer research team via Tech Fleet; cross-functional partners across product, engineering, and design

Kadena's blockchain platform had a structural problem: its multi-chain architecture was genuinely novel, but the onboarding documentation assumed mental models developers didn't have. New contributors were encountering the platform's chain indexing and Pact smart contract language without the conceptual scaffolding to make sense of either. The result was early drop-off that looked like a motivation problem but was actually a communication failure.

Working as part of a volunteer research team across product, engineering, and design, we ran a large-scale survey (100+ respondents) to map where developer confidence collapsed during onboarding. The survey wasn't a satisfaction instrument — it was designed to locate the specific moments where the platform's architecture conflicted with user mental models.

Survey ResearchUsability TestingWorkflow MappingSynthesis WorkshopsDeveloper UXMental Model AnalysisBlockchain / Web3
What problem were you solving?
Kadena needed to understand why developer drop-off during onboarding was high despite strong platform capabilities. The hypothesis was documentation quality; the reality was more specific — the documentation was technically accurate but written for someone who already understood what made Kadena different.
What did you find?
Chain count as cognitive anchor: developers consistently anchored on Kadena's 20-chain architecture as the first point of confusion. Confidence gap at Pact introduction: developer confidence dropped most sharply where Pact (Kadena's smart contract language) was introduced without a bridge from familiar paradigms.
What was the impact?
Follow-up evaluative research after recommendations were implemented measured a 28% reduction in user confusion and an 18% decrease in task completion time for beginner users. The K-Block blockchain explorer shipped and is publicly accessible.
What would you do differently?
I would push earlier for a longitudinal component: tracking the same cohort of developers through their first 30 days rather than measuring comprehension at a single point, since a snapshot survey misses the compounding friction that causes drop-off weeks later.
